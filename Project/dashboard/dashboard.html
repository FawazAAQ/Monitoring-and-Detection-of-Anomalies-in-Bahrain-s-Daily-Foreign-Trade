How anomalies are detected

The system processes Bahrain's daily foreign trade declarations through three stages: cleaning, a unified anomaly pipeline that scores each row along three dimensions, and two parallel LLM enrichment passes for human-readable explanation and CR portfolio review.

Each row is evaluated independently along three dimensions: price, trade pattern, and CR profile. The final severity is the maximum of the three sub-levels, ordered NORMAL < LOW < MEDIUM < HIGH. The final_reason column records which dimension(s) drove the level — for example, price+cr_profile means both flagged at the same maximum severity.

Anomaly dimensions

Price. Robust z-score and IQR fences computed within HS-by-month peer groups. Outliers are scored relative to the median absolute deviation; floor of 1e-6 applied to MAD to prevent underflow when peer prices are near-identical.

Trade pattern. Joint detection of partner rarity, route rarity, and burstiness. Scores reflect how unusual the partner-route-time combination is relative to historical norms for that HS code.

CR profile. CR-level behavioural features — first-time partner detection, HS portfolio drift, burst severity vs. own-history baseline, and price deviation vs. own peer-group history.

Severity levels

High
Strong anomaly on at least one dimension. Requires immediate analyst review.
Medium
Notable deviation. Should be reviewed during the daily triage cycle.
Low
Mild deviation. Logged for trend monitoring; usually no immediate action.
Normal
Within expected ranges across all dimensions. No flag raised.

LLM explanation layer

For flagged items, an LLM (GPT-4o) reads the row's quantitative features and produces a plain-language explanation, a confidence rating, a validation reasoning trace, and a recommended next investigative step. Outputs are pre-computed at pipeline time and served as static text — no live LLM calls happen during dashboard browsing.

A separate LLM check (CR-LLM) compares each CR's registered ISIC4 activities against the HS codes appearing in their declarations, producing a per-CR risk verdict (LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH) for portfolio-mismatch detection.

Refresh model

The dashboard is intentionally non-live. New pipeline output appears only when an analyst or admin clicks Refresh. If new data is detected, the previous state is archived to the snapshot history and the dashboard reloads with the new data. This protects against partially-written CSVs and lets analysts work against a stable view during their review session.
